Why this book exists

Four jurisdictions have put dates on the post-quantum migration: US federal key establishment by 2030 and signatures by 2031, the UK complete by 2035, the EU and Australia on their own clocks. The standards those deadlines point at — FIPS 203, 204 and 205 — were published in 2024. The nearest thing to a canonical textbook was published in 2009, before the NIST competition existed.

This book closes that gap, and it closes it by building rather than asserting. It starts at modular arithmetic and ends inside the standards themselves, read clause by clause. Nothing is assumed beyond undergraduate algebra, matrix arithmetic, and the ability to read Python.

What you get

  • Why Shor breaks RSA and ECDH but leaves lattices standing — and why “Grover halves AES” is misleading in practice
  • Lattices, SIS and LWE, and the number-theoretic transform, built from nothing
  • ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA and FALCON, derived line by line against the standards
  • Code-based and hash-based alternatives given equal weight, not a footnote
  • How to verify a NIST security category rather than trust one
  • 196 exercises with complete solutions, and 16 runnable labs whose every numerical claim is tested in CI

Who it is for

  • Security engineers with a migration to deliver and hard questions to answer about it
  • Postgraduates entering the field who need the mathematics properly, not by allusion
  • Lecturers building a module — the book maps onto a twelve-lecture semester, with a reading map included
  • Decision-makers — Chapters 1, 15 and 16 are a complete, self-contained forty-page argument about what is true, what it costs, and how much of the urgency is real

Honest about what nobody can tell you

“Hard” is a claim about the best published attack, not a theorem. This book says so, repeatedly, and shows it happening: the Castryck–Decru break of SIDH, and — three weeks before this book went to press — the withdrawal of a NIST Round 3 signature candidate after a key-recovery attack found by an AI system and confirmed by a human within hours.

Several claims in this book are anchored to standards that were still moving at press time. Every affected chapter carries a dated note, and the standards tracker maintains the live status of all of them — so a reader with any printing can see exactly what has changed since.
