THE SIX NEW ISSUES (all dated 2026-10-30)
------------------------------------------
1. The Maths Underneath #7 — "The arrow that points uphill"
   The derivative as a ratio that stops shrinking, the five rules that
   cover machine learning, partials, and the gradient every training
   step walks against. Notebook: 05_derivatives_gradients.ipynb. 6 min.

2. The Expensive Answers #7 — "Why vector databases choose availability"
   (from Post_07, essentially verbatim)
   CAP for working architects: retrieval was never exact, stale beats
   unavailable, the index is derived data, freshness SLO not consistency
   pretence. Uses cap.png as fig-07-the-cap-triangle.png. 4 min.

3. Amplitudes #6 — "The three families that run everything"
   Hermitian (real eigenvalues, the measurable numbers), unitary (the
   length-preservers every gate must be), projectors (P² = P, the squared
   shadow is the probability). NumPy-only, as issues 1–5. 6 min.

4. Six Ideas #6 — "The multiplication that saves lives"
   Half-life sets the dosing sawtooth, thirty doublings hide a tumour,
   and 10⁻⁶ × 10⁻⁶ = 10⁻¹² is why combination therapy wins, with the
   Frei–Freireich story. 6 min.

5. Attention, Derived #6 — "Where 14336 comes from"
   The FFN as a key-value memory, GeLU's non-monotone dip, gating as a
   degree-2 interaction (not an activation function), and the four-step
   arithmetic behind intermediate_size 14336, worth 19.2% of the model. 7 min.

6. After Shor #6 — "What hard actually promises"
   SVP, CVP, approximation factors: NP-hard near γ = 1, unlikely past √n,
   the standards in the polynomial gap; BDD's λ₁/2 uniqueness radius; and
   what the worst-case-to-average-case reduction does and does not buy. 8 min.
